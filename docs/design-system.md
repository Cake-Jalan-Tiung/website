# Design system

How the brand is built in code: values, rules, theme, primitives and components, and the decisions behind them. For the site as a whole, see the [README](../README.md).

The brand from the Paper file "Cake Jalan Tiung" (pages Concept, Grammar and Mock up), built on [Astryx](https://astryx.atmeta.com) 0.7.17 with Vite and React 19. Where Paper disagrees with itself, the Grammar page wins.

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm dev        # TanStack Start on the Workers runtime: site at /, menu at /menu, design system at /design-system, worst case at /stress
pnpm db:migrate:local   # once, after copying .dev.vars.example to .dev.vars
pnpm test       # brand rules, order rules (prices, pick-up times, status moves) and the CHIP signature check
pnpm spec       # rendered components against Paper's measurements, in headless Chromium
pnpm fit        # every page at six widths; fails if anything overflows
pnpm export     # the social posts and the receipt header as PNGs at the size they are used, into exports/
python3 scripts/print-colours.py   # CMYK for coated stock and its error, into src/system/print.generated.ts
pnpm build
```

## How it is layered

Each layer reads only the ones above it.

1. **Values** (`src/system/tokens.ts`). Every brand value, written once: colours, the six type steps, spacing, the sheet scaffold, stroke weights, minimum sizes. The theme publishes them as `--cjt-*` custom properties, and the model, the marks generator and the specimen read the same objects. Nothing else in the repo states a brand colour, type step or spacing step; `components.module.css` holds only each component's own Paper measurements.
2. **Rules** (`src/system/model.ts`). The brand as data:
   - **Grounds and inks:** every surface the brand uses, and which text colour may sit on which (`legalText`). `pnpm test` checks every pair clears 4.5:1, every forbidden pair stays below it, and every contrast figure Paper prints.
   - **Law 04 engines:** `Stage` will not type-check an edible drawing on buttercream, and the marks registry is held to the same law by a test.
   - **Print** (`src/system/print.ts`): how each palette colour reaches paper. `scripts/print-colours.py` converts the printed colours to CMYK with Adobe's Coated FOGRA39 profile and measures each against the screen colour (CIEDE2000) into `print.generated.ts`; `print.ts` holds the choices made from those numbers. The Colour chapter prints them under the swatches.
3. **Theme** (`themes/cjt/`). Sets Astryx's own components to the brand through the theme editor: cobalt accent, true greys, white page, square corners, Jost, no shadows. `cjt.theme.ts` adds the tokens from layer 1 and nothing else. A test holds every colour in the editor's settings to `tokens.ts`.
4. **Primitives** (`src/system/`):
   - `Field` paints a flat field of one brand colour and tells what sits inside which ground it is on. Everything coloured is a `Field`.
   - `Type` sets one of the six steps. Its ink defaults to the ground's primary voice, and in development it logs an error for an ink that is illegal on its ground.
   - `Page`, `Sheet`, `Band`, `Bar`, `Marquee`, `Stack`, `Row`, `Columns` lay pages out on the brand's spacing steps. A page is full-width bands and gutter-held sheets, one under another. `Page` keeps the header, the bar and the sign-off outside `<main>`. `Bar` stays at the top on one line and the page keeps anchors clear of it. `Marquee` passes its content in a loop; it stops under the pointer, on a tap and from its own keyboard button, and under reduced motion it stands still and scrolls by hand.
   - `Stage` takes a `mark` to fill it at its drawn height; a stage grows for anything taller.
   - `ink="secondary"` on any text is the ground's receding voice: muted on paper, ink at 60% where muted fails, warm grey on ink.
5. **Brand components** (`src/system/components.tsx`, metrics in `components.module.css`):

| Component | From Paper |
| --- | --- |
| `Label` | Grammar 04 small capitals |
| `Statement`, `PageHeader` | Concept statement and masthead |
| `SectionHeader`, `Rule` | Concept and Grammar section header; the hairline and the ink rule |
| `Stage`, `Tile`, `MarkTile` | Concept tiles; Law 04 grounds per drawing |
| `Verdict` | Grammar Correct / Never example tiles |
| `RuleList` | Grammar 02 custody rows (Always / Never) |
| `ColourRoles`, `Swatch` | Grammar 05 roles; Concept palette |
| `FlavourCard` | Grammar 06 flavour cards |
| `Logo`, `TypesetWordmark`, `Tiung` | Size-aware and ground-aware marks (Grammar 04, 14). `Logo stacked` is the tall lockup; `TypesetWordmark mono` is ink only, for one-ink print |
| `BilingualLabel`, `StackedPair` | Grammar 15 middot pair and stacked pair |
| `MenuBoard`, `Price`, `PinkDot` | Mock up 03 menu board |
| `Artboard`, `ArtboardTile` | Ours: a piece drawn at a fixed size, and that piece shrunk to fit a chalk stage |

6. **Pages.** Built from layers 4 and 5; they hold no styling of their own beyond a few specimen-only measurements. Copy lives in `src/site/content.ts`, and no page states a brand line that is not in it or in Paper.
   - **The site** (routes `/` and `/menu` → `src/site/`) is an F&B storefront in English, built phone first like an ordering app rather than the brand book: one slim sticky header (bird and wordmark, Menu, Best sellers, Visit, and Order now or the bag with its count and total as a compact ticket; under 900px the bird without the wordmark; on a phone only the bird and Menu, with Order now and the bag at the foot of the screen instead), a hero whose picture is the shopfront drawn in the brand's colours (below; first on a phone), whose door opens onto the counter: the dishes that have drawings, as a menu to order from with the Google rating, the promise and one full-width Order for pick-up (delivery is in the visit band, the menu and the footer), four facts, the best sellers as cards with + on each photo, photo tiles per category, the season's dish when there is one, the visit band (hours by day, address, phone, Google Maps, pick up or delivery), and a footer. The menu page is a short intro, a category rail that stays under the header and marks the category in view, the best sellers (a row a thumb swipes on a phone, four across on a wide screen), then every category as hairline rows: name, two lines of description and price beside the photo with + on its corner. One tap on + adds a dish; once it is in the bag the corner shows the count. A tap anywhere else on a dish opens it in a bottom sheet (`src/storefront/sheet.tsx`): photo edge to edge, the whole description, a quantity, and Add, Update or Remove. While the bag holds anything, a ticket pinned to the foot of the screen shows View bag with the count, the soonest pick-up and the total. On a phone's home page with an empty bag the same bar offers Order for pick-up and whether the shop is open, once the hero's button has scrolled out of sight. Pick-up orders are placed and paid on the site (`src/orders/`, plan in `docs/checkout-plan.md`): the bag lives in the browser; `/checkout` is focused (no ticker, no nav) and runs the bag with steppers, then three numbered steps (pick-up day and time on the phone's own picker, details with autofill, way to pay), with Pay pinned to the foot of the screen; a returning customer's details and way to pay are remembered; a Pay that cannot go yet takes the customer to the step that stops it. The payment provider is CHIP (or a local test page with `PAYMENTS=fake`), `/order/<id>` follows the order on a field in its status's colour (cobalt only for Ready) under the order code at display size, and the kitchen works it on `/staff`. Delivery still links to the Beep store. Both pages compose `src/storefront/` (below) with the shop's content from `content.ts`.
   - **The design system** (route `/design-system` → `src/specimen/DesignSystem.tsx`) is laid out as a brand book: a cobalt cover, the statement beside the bird, nine chapters that each open with a display title and the brand's own line, the grounds as full-width fields named for the objects they came from, the menu board on ink, then the applications: packaging, social and the counter, each piece an `Artboard` at its drawn size. Inside the chapters the components keep Paper's measurements.
   - **The worst-case page** (route `/stress` → `src/specimen/Stress.tsx`) is dev only.
   - **The export page** (route `/export` → `Exports` in `src/specimen/Applications.tsx`) is dev only: each exportable piece unscaled, for `pnpm export` to photograph at its own scale (3× for social, 576 / 272 for the receipt header). It checks every PNG against the size the piece says it is used at.

**Storefront** (`src/storefront/`) is the reusable layer for F&B sites. `model.ts` is the shape of a shop (name, address, hours, announcements, nav, an order link per fulfilment, categories that hold their dishes, best sellers, the season's dish if any; `model.test.ts` holds the ids together); `sections.tsx` renders each section from that shape alone, through layers 4 and 5. Another client fills the same shape and brings its own brand layer (values, theme, marks); the sections still name this brand's marks and wordmark, so that layer is swapped with them.

**Marks** (`src/marks/marks.generated.tsx`) are 17 React components generated from the SVGs Paper exported (`assets/marks/`) by `scripts/build-marks.mjs`. Each brand colour reads a `--cjt-mark-*` slot before its token, so a mark can be recoloured, as the cream-on-cobalt logo is, without editing paths. Each registry entry names the ground Paper shows it on.

## The checks

- **`pnpm spec`** holds 31 measurements copied from Paper's computed styles plus a check that Jost really loaded. Each row names its source sheet and layer and is checked on every matching element. It fails on a 1px difference. It proves those properties only; a full visual diff against Paper needs an export once Paper's limit resets.
- **`pnpm fit`** loads the home page, menu, checkout, kitchen screen, specimen and worst-case page at 320, 390, 768, 900, 1024 and 1440 wide. It fails if any element leaves the viewport, any box holds content wider than itself, or the console logs an error, which includes an illegal ink on a ground.
- **`pnpm test`** is the model: pairings, Law 04, the wordmark on every ground, and the theme's colours.
- **`pnpm walk`** (against a running dev server: `WALK_URL=http://localhost:5179 pnpm walk <dir>`) orders on a phone from home to paid: menu, the rail, the dish sheet, adding and removing, an empty Pay, a cancelled payment, an order waiting for payment, and a returning customer's paid order. It saves a screenshot of each screen, checks each for spills at 390 and 320 wide with the same check as `pnpm fit`, and fails on a spill, a console error or a broken step. With `WALK_STAFF_PIN` set it also signs in to `/staff` and walks the order to Ready. `WALK_BROWSER=webkit` walks it in Safari's engine (WebKit) instead of Chromium, once it is installed with `pnpm exec playwright-core install webkit`.

## Changing things

- A brand value: edit `src/system/tokens.ts`, then `pnpm exec astryx experimental theme compile themes/cjt/cjt.theme.ts`. A colour also needs `pnpm marks`, and a printed colour `python3 scripts/print-colours.py`.
- An Astryx dial or part: `pnpm exec astryx experimental theme editor set cjt <thing> <value>`, then `... editor show cjt --png` to look.
- A pairing: edit `legalText` in `model.ts`. The tests decide whether it is legal.
- A component measurement: change `components.module.css`, then the matching row in `scripts/spec-check.mjs` if Paper changed, then `pnpm spec`.
- A photo: drop it into `src/assets/photos` named after its id, then `pnpm photos`. `pnpm test` fails until it is sized.
- A mark: re-export the SVG from Paper into `assets/marks/`, update its row in `scripts/build-marks.mjs`, run `pnpm marks`.
- An application: edit its entry in `src/specimen/Applications.tsx`, then `pnpm fit` (an artboard that clips its content fails it) and, for a social piece, `pnpm export`.
- Before shipping: `pnpm test && pnpm spec && pnpm fit && pnpm exec astryx doctor --built src`.

## Decisions

- **Light mode only.** Paper defines no dark palette; the reversed marks cover dark grounds.
- **Square corners and no shadows** (Grammar 05: "gradients, glows and drop shadows contradict the entire premise"). Pills such as switches keep `--radius-full`.
- **Jost everywhere,** loaded by the theme. Paper sheets set in system-ui were drift (Grammar 04 says so itself).
- **Type follows Grammar 04,** not the off-scale sizes some sheets use (20, 28, 32px). The one exception is the menu board, which matches Mock up 03 exactly (names 24/26 Medium −0.03em, descriptions 13/17), because the mock is the only drawing of it.
- **Astryx headings keep Astryx sizes** except h1 (40) and h3 (Subhead 24/28 Regular). Brand headings use `Type` (`head`, `display`), so dialog and drawer titles are not blown up to 40px. Display scales from 68px at 1440 wide down to 45px on phones.
- **Secondary text in Astryx components is `#6D6D6D`.** Astryx's contrast check refused `#767676` on its muted surfaces. Brand components keep `#767676`, and only on white. Where Paper set muted text on chalk (the Never tag), it is ink at 60% instead, which clears 4.5:1.
- **Colour fields are `Field`, never an Astryx `Card` variant.** One way to paint a ground means one place that knows the ground, so text on it can be checked.
- **Astryx buttons follow Grammar 05.** "All link and button text" is cobalt, so secondary is a cobalt hairline with cobalt text and ghost is cobalt text. Primary is cream on cobalt, as on the awning. Paper drew no buttons; these are the roles applied, not a copy of a drawing.
- **Sections open with the marker rule:** cobalt, at the 7px weight of a drawn contour. Grammar 05 gives every structural line to the Artline marker, and the weight is the marks' own.
- **Both pages' compositions are ours.** Paper has no page mock beyond the menu board, and its sheets are quiet grids. The pages use only system components, values and the brand's own lines, and get their scale from the drawings and the colour fields, because the type stops at 68px.
- **The cream at step 400 is `cream-400` everywhere,** the name Grammar 05 gives it. On ink the primary text colour is cream (Mock up 03), and on the cobalt awning only cream is legal, because that is all Paper sets on it.
- **Brand components are plain HTML and CSS modules, not Astryx primitives.** Astryx's layout components only take spacing in 4px steps up to 40px, and Paper's measurements (18, 22, 26, 28, 44, 56px) and scaffold (96, 128px) aren't on that grid. Astryx's doctor flags this as style warnings, which are deliberate here. Astryx's own components (Button, TextInput, Link and the rest) are themed and used as-is.
- **Status colours are Astryx's defaults.** Pandan, gula and ais are too pale to be visible as fills.
- **The cream-on-cobalt logo is a reconstruction** (colour slots swapped) until the shopfront's own artwork can be exported.
- **The applications are our compositions.** Paper's only mock exported so far is the menu board. Each piece applies the rules it names in its caption, takes every word it prints from `content.ts`, and draws nothing new: the tote is two rectangles and a strap loop, not a drawing. Bakes appear only on cream-100 inside a typed `Stage`, so Law 04 holds there as it does on the pages.
- **Print pieces are drawn at real size,** in mm, where a CSS px is 1/96 inch, so the six steps print at 0.75pt per px (label 8.25pt, body 11.25pt, display 51pt). The sizes are common stock sizes, chosen here, not quoted by a supplier: an 8-inch cake box (203mm), a 50mm square seal, one-inch (25mm) satin ribbon, a 90 × 54mm name card, an 80mm thermal roll printing 72mm, and a 380 × 420mm canvas tote with a 280mm strap drop.
- **Cobalt is printed as ink, never as a dyed material.** In CMYK on coated stock it lands 14 CIEDE2000 from the screen colour (about 2 is visible at a glance), so the kraft box and the tote take it as a spot ink mixed to its Lab target (31 62 −108, D50) and approved as a drawdown; the box is one ink because the lockup's cream is the board itself, and the tote is natural canvas so the ink, not a stock fabric, carries the colour. Digital pieces (seal, price card) fall back to CMYK and print duller, which their captions say. Pink is Pantone 2038 C, the nearest non-fluorescent match colorxs.com lists (ΔE 2.8 on screen); 812 C is nearer but is one of Pantone's neons, which fade. Text black is 100 K.
- **Social pieces are drawn at 360px wide and posted at 3×** (1080 × 1350 post, 1080 × 1920 story), so the six type steps hold at the size a phone shows them. The story keeps the top and bottom 250px clear, the margin third-party guides give for Instagram's own buttons; Meta's own figure was not checked.
- **The receipt follows the till.** An 80mm printer prints 576 dots across 72mm at 203 dpi (Epson's TM-T82 brochures) in one ink and sets the lines in its own font, so the brand's part is the header image (`pnpm export` writes it at 576 dots, no grey and no outline) and the words. The mock shows the lines in Jost for reading. The total line is Jumlah · Total, the Malay word for a sum before the English, as sheet 15 orders every pair; it is in `labels`, though not from Paper.

- **The storefront menu is the Beep menu,** copied with its names, descriptions, prices, sections and Best Seller list on 23 November 2026. It is not live: a change on Beep needs the same change in `content.ts`. The Paper menu (`menu`) stays for the design system's menu board, which draws the mock.
- **Photos are found by file name.** A file in `src/assets/photos/` named after a dish or category id (or `hero`, `shop`) shows wherever that id appears; `photos.ts` does the lookup and the folder's README lists the names. Media sit in fixed-ratio boxes (dishes 1:1, categories and the shop 4:3), so a grid holds its rhythm while photos arrive. For now every photo is Beep's own product photo, downloaded on 23 November 2026 at 800px and saved as JPEG (about 150 KB each), and served as below; the hero and the category tiles borrow a dish's photo (`heroCover`, `cover`) until they have their own. Without a photo a dish shows its drawing (Shio Pan, Croissant, Butter Tteok, Latte, Long Black), otherwise the wordmark on cream-400; never a borrowed drawing.
- **The storefront is in English** at the owner's request. It does not use the Malay-first `labels` or "Habis bila habis", which the brand never translates; the design system keeps both.
- **Hours, phone and rating come from the Google Maps listing** "Cake Jalan Tiung Seksyen 4", read on 23 November 2026: 8am to 6pm, closed Thursdays; 4.2 from 704 reviews. The rating is linked to the listing and `storefront.proof` removes it everywhere. No review text is copied.
- **`Price` prints sen only when there are any:** 12, 16.50. The storefront says the currency (RM 12, RM 16.50) because a customer is about to pay; checkout, the bag and receipts always print two decimals (RM 16.50, RM 12.00).
- **No season band** until the shop names a seasonal dish; `storefront.inSeason` turns it on.
- **The storefront is denser than the brand book:** its own header and section container, a 64px step between sections, no marker rules. `Sheet`, `Band` and `Bar` are unchanged for the design system.
- **The storefront's commerce controls are its own buttons, not Astryx's.** Every touch target there is 48px (`--store-tap`): +, the count, the stepper's cells, the rail, Pay and the bag bar. Astryx's Button runs 28 to 32px at the sizes the site used, and resizing it through the theme would also resize the staff board and the design system. So `src/storefront/bag.tsx` draws them as native buttons on the brand's own values: `Cta` is the one big action on a screen (a ticket, below), `Stepper` is one hairline frame around three 48px cells. This departs from AGENTS.md and from using Astryx's components as they are, and `astryx doctor` reports more style findings for it (539 before, 625 after); it fails nothing. The bottom sheet, banners, inputs and links are still Astryx's.
- **On a landscape laptop window the hero is the street.** From 1240px wide at 5:3 or wider (1280×720, 1366×768, 1440×800, 1536×864, 1920×1080 browser windows), the first screen is Jalan Sukun itself: the shop stands at full height against the right edge, unframed, and the street (`Street` in `src/site/Shopfront.tsx`) fills the rest behind the words. It is the neighbour's wall, washed in the hero's own cream-100 so the words sit on it as if painted there and keep their legal inks, with a drainpipe at the join, the road's name plate in cobalt (taken from the address: JLN SUKUN 4/7 · SEKSYEN 4), the meter box and its cable, a high window, a potted plant and a painted plinth; the five-foot way, kerb and road run on from the shop's at the same heights. It is drawn in the shop's units (1100 tall) and anchored to its edge, so the bands meet exactly and a narrower window crops the far end. The words centre on the wall above the plinth, from the header's gutter. The rubber tree's leaves reach over the join, so the scene clips only while the street falls away going in and until it is back (`transition-behavior: allow-discrete` on `overflow`); inside, the counter takes the shop's place while the wall and its Order stay. The hint moves onto the road under the door. 1240px and 5:3 keep at least 640px of wall for the words; a squarer or narrower window keeps the framed picture beside the words, sized to the screen's height so it never falls past the fold. `pnpm fit` checks the street at three laptop windows and fails if the hero does not end exactly at the fold. Phones keep the drawing first, full width. The street's raw SVG raised `astryx doctor`'s style findings from 803 to 846; it fails nothing.
- **What is pinned to the foot of the screen reserves its own height.** The bag bar, the last-order card and checkout's Pay each report their measured height (`src/storefront/foot.ts`, a `ResizeObserver` writing `--foot-bar`, `--foot-card` and `--foot-pay` on `<html>`). The page adds them to `scroll-padding-bottom`, so a control reached by Tab stops above them. The footer and the checkout form keep that much room below their last line. A constant could not do this: Pay grows with an error above it, and everything grows with larger text. Keyboard walks after the change found no focused control hidden on `/menu` (85 stops), home with the bag and card (37) or checkout at 1440×800 (18).
- **Layouts follow the reader's text size.** The street hero's breakpoint is `77.5em`, and the phone/wide split for the bag (the foot bar against the header ticket) is `37.5em`. These are 1240px and 600px at the browser's usual 16px text. A reader with larger text keeps the simpler layout longer instead of getting words spilled over the plinth or a cut navigation.
- **A control keeps focus while it cannot act.** Pay while busy, and the stepper at either end, say so with `aria-disabled` and ignore the press instead of `disabled`, which drops focus to the page. Each change to the bag is announced once in a status line in the header, but not when a page opens.
- **The category rail says when there is more.** When categories wait past the rail's edge, a paper square with an arrow sits at its end and moves the rail on when tapped. It is for the pointer, since every category has its own link, so it steps aside while the keyboard is in the rail. A fade would have been the usual cue, but the brand has no gradients.
- **Focus rings stay readable on what they cross.** Footer links are cream on ink. The + turns its whole 48px square paper under an ink ring. The door's ring sits on its own wood, the rail's inside its ink bar, and the bag bar's on its cream body. Text fields gain a cobalt ring on focus, set through the theme editor on those two parts only, because a border changing from ink to cobalt alone was a hue change. The warning banner's fill is teh, set through the theme, so a warning never looks like a ticket.
- **The last order rises as a card, never a strip.** A returning customer's last order (`src/storefront/again.tsx`) rises from the foot of the screen 700ms after the first browsing page of a visit settles: paper in an ink hairline, the dishes' photos stacked like plates, what it was, and the ticket as its stub (Order again with the total, or, while the order is in the kitchen, its status, the order page's steps as thin bars and Track). It does not block the page or take focus. Close, Escape or a swipe down puts it away for the visit (`sessionStorage`); until then it waits settled on each browsing page reached in place. On a phone it stands in for the idle Order for pick-up and rises above the bag bar, so the foot holds one thing plus the bag; wider, it sits in the bottom corner. It replaced a static strip under the header that pushed every page down. A modal was ruled out: an interstitial on arrival is dismissed by reflex, and Google ranks pages with intrusive mobile pop-ups lower.
- **Actions are counter tickets.** Every big action (Order for pick-up, Add, Pay, Order again, I'm here) is one shape: a cream-400 field with what it does on the left in regular weight and, when it costs something, the amount on the right in medium figures with RM in small capitals, then a 48px cobalt square holding the arrow. 48px tall. The bar at the foot of the screen is the same ticket, 56px, torn along a 1px dashed ink edge, its words in small capitals over the soonest pick-up. Cobalt is the arrow's square alone, so the colour of the awning marks the step to take without filling the screen; on a cobalt ground (an order that is Ready) the square turns ink. This replaced full-width cobalt slabs with one centred line of cream text, which the owner found heavy. + on a photo is a 32px square set 8px in from the corner, the button's own padding, so the finger still gets 48px; in the bag it turns cream-400 with an ink hairline and the count. Three directions were drawn side by side before choosing (keyline on cobalt, ink and pink, the ticket); the owner chose the ticket and asked for it thinner.
- **The counter is a menu** (`src/storefront/counter.tsx`, its dishes named by id in `storefront.counter` in `content.ts`). Each cell is a dish's own drawing with + on its corner, its name (which opens the dish sheet) and its price, three across on ink hairlines (two on a phone), bakes before coffee; the last cell is an ink block to the full menu. Only dishes with a drawing go on it, and a test holds that; the drawings come from the dishes, so Law 04 is still checked by their type. Tea and hot chocolate have drawings but are not sold, so they are not on it.
- **The shopfront** (`src/site/Shopfront.tsx`) is the owner's photo of 9, Jln Sukun 4/7 redrawn flat: teh brick, the sign in cobalt caps on chalk concrete with rust running from it, the ink awning and blind, the gula-framed glass front with the crowned decal, stools, the Butter Tteok board, and the rubber tree in pandan with a few pink new shoots. Every colour is a brand token and every edge an ink line; no gradients, no shadows. It is decorative to screen readers except its door, which is a real button ("Open the door and see what is on the counter"). Opening it swings the door in, scales the street past the doorway and fades up the counter menu, whose dishes then arrive one after another; focus moves to "Back outside", and back to the door on return. Under reduced motion the two views simply swap. Before anyone opens it, the door nudges twice, once the page has settled, and then stays still. Its raw SVG shapes raised `astryx doctor`'s style findings to 742; it fails nothing.
- **The counter's motion is an entrance, not a loop.** The rules draw from the top left, the drawings rise into their cells one after another, their pink marks (steam, crumbs) float up last and the bird lands; all of it is over within two seconds, so nothing moves on its own for long enough to need a pause control. Under a pointer a drawing lifts and its pink marks breathe again. Under reduced motion nothing moves at all.
- **Pick-up time is the phone's own picker,** a native `select` (a wheel on iOS), with the day as chips above it. A grid of every 15-minute slot ran to about 40 chips on a phone.
- **On checkout, Pay is fixed to the foot of a phone's screen,** with any error right above it in a live region, and the page keeps fields clear of it when they are scrolled to. While a field is being typed in, Pay steps aside (hidden, not moved, so it never shifts under a tap) and returns when the field is left. Each detail is checked by the same rules the server uses (`customerProblems` in `src/orders/model.ts`) and its problem is shown on that field.
- **On a wide screen the bag is the left column of checkout and the steps the right,** so reading order, tab order and the page order agree.
- **The order page's field is cobalt only for Ready.** Paid and Preparing sit on its pale wash, blue-50; cream-400 is not used there because it is all but the colour of the warning banner.
- **Text inputs and text areas have ink borders** (`--color-border-emphasized` set to ink on those two parts only, through the theme editor). The theme's grey border was 1.48:1 on white, under the 3:1 a control's edge needs.
- **Photos are served at the size they are shown.** `pnpm photos` (`scripts/build-photos.mjs`, ImageMagick) writes each photo at 400 and 800px wide (never wider than the original, and named for the width it has) as WebP into `src/assets/photos/sized/`, every run, and every img offers both in a `srcset` with the `sizes` of its box, so a 120px menu row fetches the 400px file even at 3×. The menu scrolled to its end went from 3.9 MB of photos to 448 KB (measured in headless Chromium at 390px, 2× and 3×). The originals stay as the sources. `photos.test.ts` fails while a photo has no sized pair, and until then the page shows the original. The first two best sellers on the menu load at once; everything else loads as it nears the screen.
